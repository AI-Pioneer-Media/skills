# Elsewhere Article

Write an original Chinese feature article with a clear governing tension, human stakes, factual discipline, and restrained emotional force. Abstract the editorial principles; never copy another publication's signature phrases or impersonate its author.

Before drafting a full article, read [references/editorial-rubric.md](references/editorial-rubric.md) completely.

## Protect the source

- Separate verified facts, source claims, estimates, interpretations, and unknowns.
- Attribute claims to named sources. Preserve qualifications and conflicting evidence.
- Never invent scenes, quotations, motives, emotions, chronology, or first-person experience.
- When source material is insufficient, state the missing support and ask only for information that would materially change the article.
- If the input is a URL, use the appropriate extractor or browser before writing. Remove page chrome and keep the clean source.

## Build the hidden editorial map

Do not draft from the top of the notes. First create an internal map with six fields:

1. **Human carrier** — a named person or a specific affected role.
2. **Desire** — what this person or group wants.
3. **Resistance** — what blocks, contradicts, or complicates that desire.
4. **Choice and cost** — what is refused, risked, lost, or paid.
5. **Governing tension** — the one contradiction that explains the material.
6. **Earned meaning** — the larger conclusion supported by concrete choices and consequences.

Choose one governing sentence before writing:

- Person: `为了得到 X，他主动放弃或承担了 Y。`
- System: `越是 A，反而越是 B。`
- Event: `表面发生的是 A，真正改变的是 B。`

If no sentence fits without distortion, the material does not yet have a coherent article. Gather more evidence or narrow the subject.

## Choose the narrative mode

### Person-centered material

- Define the person through decisions, refusals, tradeoffs, and changes of mind.
- Prefer revealing actions and exact language over narrator praise.
- Let values become credible only after showing their price.
- Preserve oral roughness when it carries personality; do not polish every quote into corporate prose.

### Technology or system-centered material

- Do not let products, parameters, institutions, or abstractions become the only subjects.
- Translate every major technical change into a concrete person's new ability, dependency, loss, or dilemma.
- Use one or two specific roles rather than generic categories such as “用户” or “行业”.
- Make the human contradiction, not the mechanism, the article's spine.

## Prune before writing

- Keep only material that establishes the tension, proves it, complicates it, or raises its stakes.
- Rank evidence as essential, supporting, or expendable. Delete most expendable material even if it was difficult to research.
- Avoid chronological research logs unless time itself creates the drama.
- Do not give benchmarks, names, dates, and architecture details equal narrative weight.
- Resolve any contradiction between headline promise and body evidence before drafting.

## Construct the article

### Opening

Move through these beats without labeling them:

1. Enter through a concrete scene, scarce fact, consequential decision, or familiar action.
2. Reveal what is unusual or counterintuitive.
3. State the governing tension early, usually within the first 250 Chinese characters.
4. Introduce the human stakes or a line of source language with genuine character.
5. Tell the reader what the article will uncover without using promotional throat-clearing.

Do not begin with broad history, generic trend claims, or a pile of specifications.

### Body

- Use 4–8 sections. Write headings as claims, not filing-cabinet topics.
- Make every section advance the same governing tension from a different angle.
- Use the recurring pattern selectively: concrete evidence → explanation → human consequence → short landing sentence.
- Move from visible facts toward choices, costs, and finally worldview. Earn abstraction through evidence.
- Repeat one core motif only when its meaning deepens. Do not repeat slogans unchanged.
- Alternate information pressure with breathing room. Follow some long reasoning passages with a short sentence, then stop.

### Ending

- Return to the opening tension with a changed understanding.
- End on a concrete consequence, unresolved choice, or earned worldview.
- Do not append “未来已来”, “让我们拭目以待”, “快去试试”, a generic optimistic forecast, or an engagement request unless the user explicitly asks for promotional copy.
- Do not explain the final image or sentence after it has landed.

## Write with a human voice

- Prefer people and active verbs over abstract nouns and passive systems.
- Prefer plain, exact words. Let the idea carry the weight.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length. Avoid a page of equally complete explanatory paragraphs.
- Allow honest uncertainty, mixed feelings, and bounded judgment. Reserve absolute language for points the evidence can bear.
- Use first person only when the author has supplied a real experience or explicitly wants a first-person column. Never manufacture “我”.
- Use metaphors to reveal a relationship or attitude, not merely decorate or restate the explanation.
- Trust the reader. Present evidence that permits inference instead of repeatedly writing “这说明” or “由此可见”.
- Use at most one deliberately memorable landing sentence per section. Do not manufacture quotation-bait.

## Run two editing passes

### Structural pass

- Confirm the title, opening, sections, and ending all express the same governing tension.
- Confirm each section changes the reader's understanding rather than adding another pile of facts.
- Cut 20–40% when the draft reads like a complete research notebook.
- Move the strongest original insight into the opening third.

### Human-language pass

- Remove AI-like symmetry, forced three-part lists, repeated “不仅……而且……”, excessive dashes, bolding, and connective phrases.
- Replace vague authority and promotional adjectives with named sources and observable detail.
- Break uniform sentence rhythm and delete explanations that merely paraphrase the preceding sentence.
- Keep useful technical precision; humanization must not reduce factual accuracy.
- Apply the final checklist and scoring rules in [references/editorial-rubric.md](references/editorial-rubric.md).

## Deliver

Unless the user asks for planning notes, return only:

1. One recommended title, plus up to two alternatives when they offer genuinely different angles.
2. The finished article in clean Markdown.
3. A short source note only when attribution or evidentiary limits matter.

Do not expose the hidden editorial map, scoring sheet, or discarded material unless the user asks for editorial reasoning.
