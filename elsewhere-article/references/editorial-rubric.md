# Editorial rubric

Read this file before drafting or substantially rewriting an article.

## 1. Material ledger

Privately label the source material before outlining:

| Label | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Scarce fact | What can the reader not get from a generic summary? |
| Exact voice | Which verified words reveal character rather than merely convey information? |
| Decision | Who chose one path over another? |
| Refusal | What common incentive or easy path was rejected? |
| Cost | What money, time, status, control, safety, or opportunity was paid? |
| Contradiction | Which two true things appear unable to coexist? |
| Human consequence | Who can now do something, or can no longer do it? |
| Caveat | What would make the strongest claim less certain? |
| Change | What was believed before, and what becomes visible now? |

Do not fabricate missing labels. A strong article may have no quotation, but it must have a contradiction and a consequence.

## 2. Test the governing tension

A usable tension must:

- fit most of the strongest evidence;
- contain movement or opposition;
- matter to a person, not only to a category;
- survive the strongest caveat;
- lead to a conclusion more specific than “technology is changing quickly”.

Weak: `K3 is a very large model with strong benchmark results.`

Stronger: `The weights are becoming easier to obtain while the power to run them is concentrating in fewer hands.`

Weak: `The founder has a distinctive management philosophy.`

Stronger: `The larger the founder's ambition becomes, the more immediate opportunities he refuses.`

## 3. Turn information into human stakes

Replace category-only statements with a supported human consequence.

- Report: `Deployment requires 64 accelerators.`
- Human consequence: `An independent developer may download the weights but still need a cloud provider to make them move.`

- Report: `The company lowered API prices.`
- Human consequence: `The price cut gives up short-term revenue so more people can use the model.`

Do not add emotional labels such as “helpless” or “excited” unless the source supports them. The situation itself should carry the feeling.

## 4. Control narrative distance

Move deliberately among three distances:

1. **Close:** a line of speech, action, physical object, decision, or moment.
2. **Middle:** explain what happened and why it matters to the people involved.
3. **Wide:** connect the case to an industry, institution, or worldview.

Do not remain wide for several pages. Return to a person or concrete consequence after abstract explanation.

## 5. Shape emotional progression

Choose an arc supported by the material. Do not force admiration.

Common arcs:

- curiosity → contradiction → understanding → respect or unease;
- familiar event → hidden cost → changed interpretation;
- public claim → evidence → complication → more precise truth;
- personal choice → repeated proof → price paid → worldview.

The last section should carry the deepest consequence, not merely the latest fact.

## 6. Language mechanics

### Keep

- exact nouns and active verbs;
- concrete numbers when they change the reader's sense of scale;
- source language with personality;
- short verdicts earned by preceding evidence;
- modest uncertainty such as “可能”“暂时”“我还不能确定” when true;
- occasional humor or roughness that belongs to the real authorial voice.

### Reduce

- noun chains such as “生态格局演进趋势”;
- uniform `事实—解释—限定—总结` paragraphs;
- repeated transitions: “此外”“与此同时”“值得注意的是”“更重要的是”;
- automatic elevation: “标志着”“彰显了”“具有里程碑意义”;
- slogans: “未来已来”“重新定义”“开启新纪元”;
- forced contrasts: “这不仅是……更是……”;
- vague crowds: “业内人士认为”“有观点指出”;
- bold text used as a substitute for emphasis in prose;
- explaining a metaphor immediately after using it.

## 7. Detect report voice

The draft still reads like a report when:

- most sentence subjects are products, systems, parameters, markets, or abstractions;
- the author's strongest reaction never appears;
- every fact receives similar space;
- the structure follows the order of research rather than the order of revelation;
- the article answers how a system works but not who gains, loses, chooses, waits, or depends;
- the ending summarizes instead of changing the meaning of the opening.

Fix report voice by moving the governing tension forward, removing secondary evidence, and rewriting at least one paragraph per section around a supported human consequence.

## 8. Detect manufactured humanity

The draft is pretending to be human when it:

- invents an anonymous scene such as “凌晨三点，一位工程师……”;
- assigns feelings without evidence;
- adds casual slang that does not match the author's voice;
- inserts “我” without a real first-person experience;
- forces vulnerability or moral lessons;
- turns every section ending into a quotable slogan.

Remove the invention. Human stakes come from real constraints and choices.

## 9. Headline check

Before delivery, answer yes to all:

- Does the headline state the actual scope of the evidence?
- Does the opening begin fulfilling the headline within three paragraphs?
- Is the central contradiction visible rather than hidden behind a product announcement?
- Would the headline remain defensible after reading the strongest caveat?
- Does it promise an insight, not merely volume or excitement?

## 10. Final score

Score each dimension from 0 to 5:

| Dimension | 5-point standard |
| --- | --- |
| Factual integrity | Claims, attribution, scope, and uncertainty are accurate. |
| Governing tension | One contradiction organizes the whole article. |
| Human carrier | Concrete people or roles bear the consequences. |
| Choice and cost | Values and strategy are proven through tradeoffs. |
| Structural movement | Every section changes or deepens the reader's understanding. |
| Voice | The prose sounds like a thinking person, not a report or model answer. |
| Rhythm | Sentence length, pressure, and pauses vary naturally. |
| Ending | The final meaning is earned and returns to the opening tension. |

Do not deliver below 32/40. Factual integrity must score 5. Revise any dimension below 3.

Then perform a silent read-aloud test:

- Remove a sentence that explains what the reader already understands.
- Break one run of equal-length sentences.
- Replace one abstraction with an observable consequence.
- Check that the strongest sentence is supported, not merely stylish.
- Stop when the final line lands.
